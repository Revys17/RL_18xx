# Training-pipeline audit, 2026-11-08

Eight read-only reviewers audited the newest code lineage, deployment `az0-2p-0a4aeae`, plus the experiment scripts under `/home/revys/longrun/experiments`. Each covered one area:

1. policy gradient
2. search learning
3. the Rust search
4. model inputs, outputs and serving
5. value training
6. the evaluation harness
7. the experiment scripts and statistics
8. outcome and rules consistency

Most findings rest on code traces plus a small repro or a replay of saved games. The reviewers' scripts are in the session scratchpad (`review/<area>/`).

How each finding was checked:
- Two reviewers found the move limit (1) and the noise leak (8) independently, each with its own probe.
- I re-checked these against the code or the saved games myself: the starting policy (2), the eval search budget (3), the bankruptcy share (5), gen5's resume (6) and the league crediting (7). For (4), the bankrupt-winner mechanism is confirmed (that share was recounted for (5)), but the bankrupt-winner counts are the reviewer's.

## Findings that change what we do

| # | Finding | Affects | Status |
|---|---------|---------|--------|
| 1 | **The Rust search only considers the top floor(sqrt(N)) prior moves at any position with more than 20 legal moves**, at the root and deeper in the tree. At 200 readouts that is about 14 root moves, and 2-3 at small inner nodes. The threshold is hard-coded; `pw_c`/`pw_alpha` are shared with price widening. Under raw visit targets the policy gets a zero target outside the kept moves. | az0's claim to be AlphaZero; every Rust search (by design) | **Decision**: remove it for az0 (new flag, default unchanged)? |
| 2 | **The fresh network's starting policy is a confident random one.** Only the value, score and price output layers are zeroed. At track and token decisions Pass gets a prior of 1 - 1e-8 or more, and searches put only 3-6% of visits on tile lays (19-30% with noise). After decision 120 the most-visited move is played, so early games would be almost trackless. | az0 | To fix: zero the policy output layers in `make_fresh_network`. The blocks stay log-softmaxed separately, so Pass still gets ~60% at a 20-lay decision; noise and search spread visits from there. |
| 3 | **az0's searched matches had no `budget=total`.** A kept tree grew by 200 visits per consecutive move with no cap (mean 1.65x, up to 963 visits). Separately, the matches against the starting network read a path the run never writes. | az0 evals | **Fixed** in `common.sh` (budget=total; `ckpt 0` = the fresh network) |
| 4 | **A bankrupt player can be credited the win or placement points.** Stock that couldn't be sold still counts at market value in `result()`. That is Ruby's scoring, but as a training target it rewards bankruptcy. Counts: 10 of 62 bankruptcies in random 2p games; 4 of 40 in the 2p S1 vs S0 games; in 4p H_vs_G, 18 of 260 bankrupt players were not last. | every pipeline, including the trees' terminal values; az0 early on; gen5 placement | **Decided 2026-11-09: keep Ruby's scoring.** The user: a feature, since deliberately going bankrupt can win. Branch az0-bankrupt-last is abandoned; pg2p_20261109 ran with it, but it changed 2 of ~20k sampled games. |
| 5 | **Every eval since 10-31 runs under the train-shuffle rule, which none of the measured networks (gen4, its critic, lr1 v4, checkpoint 10) was trained under.** Recounted on saved games: 54% of harness H_vs_G games end in bankruptcy (129/240), against 11% for gen4-critic games before the rule (26/240). | the harness and value-swap results (still valid for today's rules, where the bot plays, but measured in a bankruptcy-heavy regime); the "vs gen4" yardsticks; gen5's comparison with gen4 (part of any gain will be adapting to the rule) | interpretation |
| 6 | **gen5's restart branch could never resume.** `--resume` had no run directory, and `--policy`/`--value` were unresolvable in this workdir. It would have restarted every 120 s forever. | gen5 | **Fixed** in run.sh: resumes from `state.json`; a crash before the first snapshot starts over |
| 7 | **League PFSP credits games to whoever held an opponent slot when the task was submitted**, not who played. Slots reload every 2 updates while tasks wait and run for several updates, so per-member win rates blur together and PFSP's weighting flattens (about 3x instead of 12x). | gen4's curriculum and its per-member rates; gen5 | To fix before gen5 (read the slot's member at game time) |
| 8 | **Root noise leaks into cq/rpo targets and into target confidence.** They read the noised prior. `_validate` even suggests cq/rpo with noise. | no launched run; any future cq + noise run | To fix: use the clean prior, or refuse the combination |

## Smaller real issues

- **The policy sampler clips the full-softmax probability at 1e-12 before tempering.** gen4 puts over 99% of its softmax mass on illegal moves (legal mass has a median of 5e-6 in Stock Rounds), so 0.1-0.4% of Stock Round decisions sample from a flattened policy. PPO's `old_logp` then disagrees with training's.
  - The same line is in `policy_gradient`, `policy_selfplay`, `search_learning`, `eval_policy_only`, `eval_head_to_head` and `rollout_targets`.
  - To fix before gen5: renormalize over the legal moves first.
- **Pooled and paired SEs treat the A-first and B-first runs as independent, but they replay the same (start, seating) cells.** SEs are 1.0-1.4x too small. No headline conclusion falls below 2 SE once clustered: harness 2v2 3.2 SE, 1v3 3.0 SE, swap +.340 ± .032. Going forward: give the B-first run its own seed, which doubles the starts covered, and cluster SEs by start.
- **The size-test verdict rests on one result**: the searched match, half vs current at .440, -2.4 SE clustered. The policy-only gap is 1.2 SE, and the speed check timed the CPU engine.
- **Who presides a non-active corporation is not encoded.** Top-holder ties occur in 43-50% of positions, so the network can't see who runs the corporation. Add it with zero-initialized weights at the next encoder change, alongside the net-worth inputs.
- **The S loops' values were never trained on auction positions**, but the policy steps search auction decisions with them.
- **Mid-game starts:** 3-4% of value positions lie in the human prefix but get the bot continuation's outcome.
- **The start-mix fractions apply one after another.** Mid-game share: loop .1625, not .25; gen5 .15, not .3.
- **Replay readers guess the auction rule instead of reading `settings.json`**: `value_gate`, `rollout_targets` eval roots and the game viewer.
- **A PG resume re-logs the updates since the last snapshot** in history.jsonl.
- **`terminal_utility` scale mismatch** for `#score`/`#blend` searches on win-reward or value-trainer networks. Not used today.
- **Price targets from explored low-visit moves are one-hot on the head's own mode.**
- **Tree reuse with 'total' makes root noise a no-op** while still counting it as noised (az2p only).
- **1v3 under the 'additional' budget is asymmetric** (latent; every reported 1v3 used budget=total).
- **`value_refit` is 4-player only**, and **rollouts' decision cap ignores auction decisions**.
- **Winner's curse:** lr1's value was promoted on the very gate sets that `rollout_targets` later compares on.
- **Provenance:** a value step's `config.json` is overwritten by its checkpoints.

## Checked and found correct

- **Seat frames and rotation:**
  - Rust and Python encoders agree for 2-6 players.
  - Every consumer unrotates values correctly.
  - Training targets are rolled into the mover's frame; checked empirically on stored loop data, with 0 mismatches.

  - Values are backed up for the right mover, including corporation presidents and auction bidders.
  - Terminal values use the share of winners, the same scale as the leaves.

- **Server replies:** packed and staged replies are exact on test requests, and dedupe_maps is correct.

  - Resume works.
